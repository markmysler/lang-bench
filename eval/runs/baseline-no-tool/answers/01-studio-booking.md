# Online class booking for your studio: what I'd use and why

## Short answer

I wouldn't write custom software for this. I'd set up a ready-made class-booking service, move your clients and timetable into it, and leave you with a one-page guide for changing times and prices.

You asked for something cheap, that doesn't break, and that you can change yourself. A ready-made service does all three better than anything I'd build. A custom system would always need someone technical to look after it, and right now that would be me. You'd be paying for that and depending on it indefinitely, for a problem other companies have already solved.

## What I'd use

You'd get a booking service built for classes. Your clients get a timetable page or link and a phone-friendly way to book and cancel. Each class has a fixed number of spots and closes when it's full, which stops the double bookings. Reminder emails go out automatically. You get a phone app or simple dashboard where you change times and prices yourself.

Shortlist within your budget:

| Service | Why it's on the list | Watch out for |
|---|---|---|
| **Bookwhen** | Built for classes and simple to run. Lower plans are usually well under $30 a month. | Fewer extras like memberships and marketing. |
| **Acuity Scheduling** (owned by Squarespace) | Reliable. Good reminders, class spot limits and class packs. Middle plans are around $16–30 a month. | Built for appointments first, so classes take a little more setup. |
| **SimplyBook.me** | Cheap, with a free tier and a class-booking feature. | The admin screens are busier and harder to learn. |

I couldn't check current prices just now; these figures are from what I already know. Prices change, so we'd confirm them before choosing.

I'd run free trials of the top two with you and pick whichever *you* find easier to use on your phone. That matters more than the feature lists.

**Card payments cost extra.** If clients pay online, the card processor (Stripe, Square or PayPal) takes about 3% per payment on top of the subscription. You could start with booking online and paying at the studio, then add online payment later.

## Alternatives I considered and ruled out

- **A custom app built by me.** Hosting could cost under $10 a month, but it's the riskiest option for you. Emails, payments, security updates and fixes would all depend on someone technical. If something broke on a Saturday morning, you couldn't fix it.
- **Studio software like Mindbody, Momence, TeamUp or Glofox.** These are excellent but usually cost $50–150+ a month or charge a fee per booking. They'd be worth a look if you grow, or if you need memberships, automatic billing and staff payroll.
- **Improving the spreadsheet** with Google Forms or locked cells. It's cheap, but it doesn't reliably stop two people taking the last spot, and it still needs fiddly upkeep.
- **Calendly or a general-purpose calendar tool.** It's fine for one-to-one sessions but weak on class packs, waitlists and a clear class timetable.

## Risks

- **Clients not switching.** Some regulars will keep emailing. You'll need a clear message, a set changeover date, and a few weeks of gently redirecting them.
- **Being tied to one company.** If prices rise you'd want to move. Before we commit, I'd check that you can export your client list and booking history.
- **Reminders landing in spam.** We'd send test bookings to Gmail, Outlook and iPhone addresses before going live.
- **Hidden costs.** Per-booking fees, text-message reminders or extra staff logins can quietly push you past $30.
- **Client data.** You'll be storing names, emails and maybe phone numbers. Depending on your country, you may need a short privacy notice. The service handles the technical side.
- **What actually causes the double bookings.** If it's two clients taking the same spot, spot limits fix it. If teachers or private sessions are clashing in your calendar, the setup changes a bit.

## What I'd check before you commit

1. **Your answers to a few questions:**
   - Which country are you in? This affects currency, tax and privacy rules.
   - Do clients pay per class, buy class packs, or pay monthly?
   - Do you want online payment now, or paying at the studio for the time being?
   - How many teachers do you have, and do they need their own logins?
   - Do you want a waitlist when a class is full?
   - What's your cancellation rule (e.g. no refund within 12 hours)?
2. **A real test during the free trial.** I'd set up a week of your actual timetable. Then you'd try changing a class time and a price on your phone without my help. If you can't do that easily, we pick the other service.
3. **The total monthly cost at your size:** about 200 clients and 300 bookings, including any fees.
4. **Whether the client list and booking history can be exported.**
5. **What happens to your data if you stop paying.**
6. **How quickly their support replies,** since you'd be the one contacting them.

## How the switch would go

- **Week 1:** trial two services with you and choose one.
- **Week 2:** I import your 200 clients from the spreadsheet, set up the timetable, prices and reminder emails, and write your one-page guide.
- **Weeks 3–4:** run it alongside the spreadsheet with a few regulars, then email everyone with a changeover date.

After that, you shouldn't need me for day-to-day running.
