# Online class booking for your studio

**My recommendation is not to build it. Buy a ready-made booking service for yoga studios and let me set it up.** I'd only build custom software if no service fits after a short trial.

## Why buying beats building

- **The hard part is a solved problem.** Double bookings happen because two people can grab the last place at the same moment. Booking services handle this reliably. A custom system can handle it too, but only if it's built and tested carefully.
- **Reminders, waitlists and payments come included.** Reminder emails that actually reach inboxes are fiddly. So are card payments, refunds and tax. Each one is something that could break in a custom build.
- **You can change prices and class times yourself.** You'd do it in a screen made for that, which the vendor supports and documents. In a custom build, I'd have to design and maintain that screen.
- **You can't debug, and a custom build needs someone who can.** If a booking silently fails at 7am, someone has to find out why. With a service, the vendor's support team does that. With custom software, it's me, and I only act when you ask me to.
- **Cost.** Services aimed at small studios often fall roughly between free and 30 USD a month. Some charge per client or take a payment fee. I haven't checked current prices, so treat that range as a guess until we verify it.

## What I'd shortlist

I'd compare 3–4 of these. Names and pricing change often, so I'd check them before we decide:

- **Momence, Walla, Punchpass, Zen Planner:** built for fitness and yoga studios, with class packs and memberships.
- **Acuity Scheduling, Bookeo, Setmore, SimplyBook.me:** more general, usually cheaper, and some handle group classes with limited places.
- **Square Appointments, or the booking add-on of a website builder such as Wix or Squarespace:** good if you already use them.
- **Mindbody:** the best-known one, but usually too expensive and too complex for a studio your size.

I'd choose based on a short checklist:

- Capacity limits per class.
- Recurring weekly timetables.
- Cancellation and waitlist rules.
- Reminder emails.
- Import of your 200 clients.
- Easy export of your data.
- A price under 30 USD.

## Alternatives I considered

- **Keep the spreadsheet and add a form or automation.** This is cheap, but it doesn't fix double bookings. Spreadsheets have no safe way to say "only one person gets the last place." It would break the same way, with more moving parts.
- **A custom build.** I'd use a managed database, a hosted website, an email service and a payment provider, with an admin page for you. This avoids running a server, and it could cost about 0–30 USD a month. I'd keep it as the fallback. Its weak points are:
  - Ongoing maintenance, such as updates, expiring services and email problems.
  - Handling client data, with the legal duties that come with it.
  - You depending on me being available whenever it breaks.
- **A no-code tool such as Airtable or Softr.** It's faster than a custom build, but the capacity handling is awkward and the monthly cost adds up. It's a middle option with the downsides of both, so I wouldn't choose it.

## Risks

- **Lock-in and price rises.** Services can raise prices or change plans. That's why I'd check that you can export your clients and bookings.
- **Clients not adopting it.** Some regulars may keep emailing you. For the first month, I'd suggest you tell them the old way is closing, or you'll end up with two systems and more double bookings.
- **Hidden fees.** Payment fees, per-client pricing and "free" plans that remove reminders or limit class counts can all push the real cost over your budget.
- **Data protection.** You'll hold client names and emails. Depending on where you are, there may be rules about consent and privacy notices. Most services help with this.
- **Me as the maintainer.** I can set things up and fix them when you ask. I don't watch the system between conversations, so it's best to choose something that needs little watching.

## What I'd check before we commit

1. **Where are you based?** This affects currency, tax, payment options and privacy law.
2. **Do clients pay online today?** If not, do you want them to start paying when they book? That changes the options a lot.
3. **How do you charge?** Is it drop-in, class packs, memberships, or a mix?
4. **What rules do you want?** For example class sizes, cancellation cutoffs and waitlists.
5. **Do you have a website or domain?** I'd want the booking page to sit on it, and reminders to come from your own address.
6. **What does the spreadsheet hold?** I'd check whether the client list is clean enough to import.
7. **A one-week trial.** Run the top two services against 3–4 real classes with a few friendly clients, before moving everyone.
